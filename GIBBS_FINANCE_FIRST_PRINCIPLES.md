# Does the Gibbs posterior work for financial markets? A first-principles analysis
*Prompted by Prof. Jiang's caution that the Gibbs posterior may not transfer to financial markets. 2026-07-19.*

## 0. The object
Gibbs (generalized-Bayes) posterior: π(θ | data) ∝ exp{ −ω·Σᵢ ℓ(θ; xᵢ) }·π₀(θ), where ℓ is an **empirical risk** (pinball/CRPS/VaR-loss), not a negative log-likelihood, and ω is the **learning rate / temperature**. It targets the *risk minimizer* θ* = argminθ E[ℓ(θ;X)], and is sold as (a) robust to model misspecification and (b) giving honest credible bands without a likelihood.

The guarantees (posterior concentration at rate, credible-set coverage) rest on four load-bearing assumptions. Financial markets break all four. Below: which assumption, why it breaks, and what it does to the posterior.

## 1. Assumption: (near-)independence / fast mixing → **BROKEN: effective sample size ≪ n**
Concentration of the empirical risk Σℓ around n·E[ℓ] is a law-of-large-numbers/Bernstein statement; it needs the summands roughly independent (or α/β-mixing fast enough). The temperature is effectively **ω·n = information count**. Financial returns are strongly dependent — volatility clustering is long-memory in |r| and r², regimes persist for months. The **effective sample size n_eff = n / (integrated autocorrelation time) is far smaller than n** (often by 5–50×, depending on the functional).

Consequence: the posterior behaves as if it has n independent observations when it has ~n_eff. It therefore **concentrates too fast and the credible bands are too tight — systematically overconfident.** This is the single most damaging failure: the headline selling point (honest uncertainty bands) is exactly what breaks, because the "honesty" is calibrated to an information count the data don't supply.

## 2. Assumption: stationarity (a fixed θ* exists) → **BROKEN: the risk minimizer drifts**
Concentration is *toward θ\* = argmin E[ℓ]*, which presumes one fixed minimizer. Markets are non-stationary: the conditional loss-distribution shifts across regimes, so **θ\* is time-varying**. Pooling all n observations to find one θ\* chases a moving target — the 2005–2024 risk-minimizer is not the 2026-Korean-crash risk-minimizer. This is deeper than misspecification (wrong shape for a fixed law); it's *no fixed law*. A posterior that averages over the whole sample is biased toward a compromise that fits no regime, and its variance understates regime risk. GARCH has the same disease at the parameter level, but at least its *conditional* variance adapts day to day; a Gibbs posterior over static θ does not.

## 3. Assumption: light/controlled tails (Bernstein condition) → **BROKEN, but pinball helps**
Concentration rates need the loss to be sub-exponential (bounded moment-generating function of ℓ). With **squared-error** risk on heavy-tailed returns (kurtosis high, arguably infinite 4th moments in crisis series), Σℓ is dominated by a few observations, √n concentration fails, and the posterior is unstable. **Pinball/quantile loss is the mitigant**: its influence per observation is bounded (∂ℓ/∂q ∈ {τ−1, τ}), so it's far more robust to heavy tails than squared error. So a *quantile*-based Gibbs posterior is much safer on this axis than a moment-based one — a genuine point in favor of the IQN/pinball framing over classical GARCH-MLE. But this fixes the tail axis, not the dependence (§1) or non-stationarity (§2) axes.

## 4. Assumption: ω is calibratable → **BROKEN under dependence**
Everything above routes through ω. Calibrating ω for correct coverage (Grünwald's SafeBayes, Syring–Martin's General Posterior Calibration, Wu–Martin) is itself built on **i.i.d.** resampling/coverage arguments. Under dependence + drift there is **no established, valid way to pick ω**, so even the "we tune ω for coverage" escape hatch rests on assumptions that fail. In practice you'd need a *block*-resampling calibration of ω that respects the dependence — which reduces the effective information and widens the bands (consistent with §1).

## 5. The deeper diagnosis: Gibbs is robust to the WRONG misspecification for finance
Gibbs's robustness is to **functional-form misspecification of the marginal, in i.i.d. data** — get the risk minimizer even if the density family is wrong. But the misspecification that actually hurts in finance is **the dynamics**: dependence, heteroskedasticity, regime-switching, non-stationarity. Gibbs offers no protection there — it *assumes it away* (i.i.d./stationary). **It solves a problem finance doesn't have and ignores the one it does.** That is, I think, the crux of Prof. Jiang's caution.

## 6. What this says about parametric vs nonparametric DISTRIBUTIONS
The resolution is about *where* you put the structure.
- **GARCH-filtering is the trick that saves you.** A GARCH/GJR model captures the dependence and heteroskedasticity in the *dynamics*; its **standardized residuals zₜ = (rₜ−μ)/σₜ are approximately i.i.d.** That's the whole point of filtering. On the *residuals*, the i.i.d. assumption is roughly restored — so a distribution model on zₜ (parametric skew-t, OR nonparametric empirical = Filtered Historical Simulation, OR a Gibbs posterior on the residual quantiles) is on much firmer ground than the same object on raw rₜ. **First-principles takeaway: do the Gibbs/nonparametric distribution work on the CONDITIONALLY-STANDARDIZED innovations, not on raw returns.** Then §1/§2 are largely neutralized by construction.
- **Parametric vs nonparametric for the innovation zₜ:** parametric (Hansen skew-t) is efficient if the residual shape is stable; nonparametric (empirical/FHS, or IQN conditioned on state) wins when the innovation shape itself is state-dependent (fat-tail-in-fat-tail, asymmetric after shocks) — which is exactly the "learnable misspecification" regime where we already see the nonparametric edge, and where residual kurtosis stays high after GARCH filtering (ARS 1937, TRY 282 — GARCH doesn't even make them i.i.d.).
- **The conditional IQN sidesteps the dependence differently:** by conditioning Q(τ|xₜ) on the vol state directly, it makes the *conditional* distribution the object — which is i.i.d.-like given the features — without a separate filtering step. So it's implicitly doing the §6 trick. That's why the ablation showed the edge comes from conditioning on recent realized vol: that conditioning IS the de-dependence.

## 7. So what should provide the UNCERTAINTY (since Gibbs shouldn't)?
For finance the honest uncertainty tools are the ones built for dependence/drift, not i.i.d.:
- **Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI; Gibbs & Candès 2021 — different "Gibbs")**: finite-sample coverage *under arbitrary distribution shift*, by online-updating the quantile level to hit nominal coverage. Purpose-built for non-stationary time series. This is the right replacement for the Gibbs-posterior's credible bands.
- **Block / stationary bootstrap** (Politis–Romano): resamples blocks to respect dependence — the correct way to get standard errors / bands on the risk forecasts.
- **Explicit state-space / stochastic-vol Bayes**: model the dynamics parametrically and be honestly Bayesian about *those* parameters.

## 8. Where Gibbs might still earn its keep (steelman)
Not zero. On **conditionally-standardized residuals within a single regime** (short, roughly-stationary window), with ω calibrated by **block resampling** and a **pinball** exponent (bounded influence), a Gibbs posterior over the residual-quantile / (VaR,ES) object could give *approximately* honest bands — and it uniquely connects to Prof. Jiang's framework and to the elicitable Fissler–Ziegel (VaR,ES) score. The honest scope is: **local, filtered, quantile-loss, resampling-calibrated** — not global, raw-return, moment-loss, i.i.d.-calibrated. Framed that way it's a defensible chapter; framed as "Bayesian VaR bands from pooled returns" it will (rightly) be shot down.

## 9. Empirical test to settle it (planned)
Directly measure whether Gibbs-posterior credible bands **under-cover** on financial data: fit a Gibbs posterior (pinball exponent) over a location-scale/VaR parameter on returns, form q-level credible intervals for next-period quantiles, and check realized coverage vs nominal — on (a) raw returns, (b) GARCH-standardized residuals, (c) with i.i.d.-ω vs block-resampled-ω. Compare against ACI coverage. **Prediction: raw-return Gibbs under-covers badly; residual + block-ω Gibbs approaches nominal; ACI is nominal throughout.** That plot *is* the argument, and it turns Prof. Jiang's caution into a result. → added to RESEARCH_IDEAS_BACKLOG.md.
